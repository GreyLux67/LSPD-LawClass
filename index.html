Constitutional Law

Learning Objectives

  • Understand constitutional protections.
  • Apply the Bill of Rights during police encounters.
  • Recognize actions that may violate civil rights.

Key Amendments

  • 1st Amendment
  • 4th Amendment
  • 5th Amendment
  • 6th Amendment
  • 14th Amendment

Search and Seizure

Reasonable Suspicion

Specific and articulable facts that reasonably suggest criminal activity may be occurring.

Probable Cause

Facts and circumstances sufficient to lead a reasonable person to believe a crime occurred or evidence will be found.

Common Search Authorities

  • Consent
  • Search incident to arrest
  • Vehicle exception
  • Search warrant

Officer Responsibility

Be able to clearly explain the legal reason for every detention, search, seizure, and arrest.

Traffic Stops

Before the Stop

  • Identify a lawful reason for the stop.
  • Notify dispatch when practical.
  • Select a safe stopping location.

During Contact

  • Use clear commands.
  • Maintain awareness of occupants.
  • Explain the reason for the stop.
  • Document enforcement action.

Miranda Rights

Miranda Is Generally Required When

  • The person is in custody, and
  • The officer conducts interrogation.

Remember

A person may be detained or arrested without immediately receiving Miranda warnings. The warning becomes important before custodial questioning.

Important Case Law

Terry v. Ohio

Recognized limited investigative stops and protective frisks when legal standards are met.

Miranda v. Arizona

Established safeguards for custodial interrogation.

Arizona v. Gant

Limited certain vehicle searches incident to arrest.

Carroll Doctrine

Permits certain warrantless vehicle searches when probable cause exists.

Scenario Discussion

Scenario

A person stands on a public sidewalk and records officers conducting a traffic stop. The person remains at a safe distance and does not interfere.


Question: Is recording alone a lawful reason to arrest the person?


Answer: No. Recording police activity in public is generally protected, provided the person does not unlawfully interfere.
